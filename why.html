Why deals die

Most broken deals fail on what diligence finds

Axial, a deal platform for the lower middle market, studied 75 deals sourced through it that broke after a signed LOI in 2025. Nearly half died on diligence findings, often after months of exclusivity.1

46.6%

of broken deals died on diligence findings: quality of earnings or otherwise1

2x

Deals killed by QoE EBITDA discrepancies rose from 10.6% in 2023 to 21.3% in 20251

106 days

$338M

Broken-deal expenses at one large private equity firm, KKR, over six years4

$25,000 to $50,000

The quality of earnings fee alone, on a $5M to $15M company

That is the typical buy-side QoE fee2, and it is paid whether or not the deal closes, though some QoE firms discount it when a deal dies3. On top of it come legal fees (a median of $28,500 on closed searcher acquisitions3), travel, lender deposits and the deal team's months in exclusivity.

QoE EBITDA discrepancies

DealBasis tests management's EBITDA bridge before you sign. On Project Saguaro it found $592,000 of Adjusted EBITDA the documents don't support, $3.55M of enterprise value at 6.0x.

Other diligence findings

Unrecorded debt, revenue reversed after period end, receivables outpacing sales and entries just under approval limits surface in the first pass, not week five.

Renegotiation

Bid on the EBITDA the evidence supports from the start, so there is less to re-trade after diligence.

Months lost in exclusivity
